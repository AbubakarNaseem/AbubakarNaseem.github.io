02 · Research

What I looked for

The objective was not to collect information for its own sake. The research focused on signals that could directly influence messaging and creative decisions.

01

Customer Problems

Repeated signs of stress at home, separation-related behavior, restlessness, barking, whining, and indoor accidents.

02

Customer Language

How dog owners naturally describe the problem, their frustration, uncertainty, and the impact on household routines.

03

Competitor Messaging

Which problems competitors emphasized, which claims were repeated, and where messaging became generic or undifferentiated.

04

Creative Patterns

Recurring hooks, visual setups, emotional triggers, and common creative structures used across the market.

03 · Key Findings

The strongest opportunities were problem-specific

The product became more relevant when the messaging connected it to concrete situations rather than relying on broad statements about calm.

Finding 01

Separation Stress

Time apart from the owner created a strong emotional context for both the dog and the customer, making it a meaningful problem-aware angle.

Finding 02

Indoor Accidents

Indoor accidents could be framed around stress and household disruption rather than immediately treating the behavior as disobedience.

Finding 03

Whining & Barking

Repeated vocal behavior gave the advertising a visible, familiar symptom that dog owners could recognize quickly.

Finding 04

Restless Home Routine

The problem affected the owner too: disrupted evenings, constant cleanup, uncertainty, and difficulty maintaining a normal routine.

05 · Creative Direction

From strategic angle to usable execution

The strategy was translated into practical guidance that could support copywriters, designers, editors, and paid media execution.

01

Hook

Start with a highly recognizable customer situation or problem.

02

Visual

Show the real household moment rather than relying only on a product shot.

03

Message

Explain the connection between stress and the behavior without overclaiming.

04

Product Role

Introduce PetStone as continuous calming support within the dog's main room.

07 · Strategic Takeaway

The research shaped the creative

The strongest advertising opportunities did not come from describing the product more aggressively.

They came from understanding the situations customers already recognize: leaving the house, coming home to an accident, hearing repeated barking, or dealing with a dog that cannot settle.

That customer context created a stronger foundation for messaging, creative concepts, and Meta ad angles.
